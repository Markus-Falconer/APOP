The idea

Ben's layout is built for easy fabrication. A straight tube bolts to the nozzle flange and the spare turbine wheel slides inside it. Two bent pipes join the tube through windows cut in its side wall, then turn back aft and neck down at the exits. The tube carries on past the windows to an end cap, and a flat-plate box behind the cap carries the motor and a slotted bearing mount. Because the bore is continuous, the wheel can slide over the windows without hitting anything; joining the pipes at the end of the tube doesn't allow that, because the 2 in pipes are smaller than the 58 mm wheel. This page models those specifics on top of the V2 engine and wheel model:

  • Window bypass from a pressure balance. Gas reaches the pipes either through the blades (and out of the window behind them) or straight out of the window ahead of the wheel. The split is set where both paths see the same pressure drop, so bypass grows smoothly as more window sits ahead of the blades.
  • Split losses. The turn into the side windows, sharp or flared window edges, the flat end cap or a cone splitter on it, the sudden expansion into the big pipes, the return bends and the necked exits.
  • Uneven loading. The split's back-pressure pattern, plus any strut wakes, swing the blade loading twice per revolution. That costs wheel efficiency and sets the forcing frequencies to check against the blade's first mode.
  • Hub treatment and guide vanes. An open cavity between the engine hub and the wheel, a hub cone on struts or on guide vanes, or a spinner on the wheel. Guide vanes pre-swirl the gas, which raises the work per kilogram, and the speed.

    5. Uneven loading and blade forcing

    The split's back-pressure pattern repeats twice per revolution, so each blade loads and unloads at 2× running speed, with harmonics at 4×, 6× and 8×. Struts add their own orders. The wheel carries only about 5% of the torque it carries in the engine, so the swing costs efficiency more than blade life. A resonance would change that, which is what the chart checks.

    Reading these charts

    The gas can reach the pipes two ways: through the blades and out of the window behind them, or straight out of the window ahead of the wheel. Both paths see the same pressure drop, so the gas splits until they balance. The wheel's own pressure drop (the work it extracts plus its losses) pushes gas toward the bypass, so a harder-working wheel passes less gas.

    Sliding the wheel aft puts more window ahead of it and less behind, so bypass grows, power and speed fall, and once the blades' mid-chord passes the windows almost nothing goes through them. Blade efficiency rises as the wheel slows, because the gas meets the blades at a less extreme angle.

    7. How the model works

    • Engine. Stock nozzle area is back-calculated from published thrust and mass flow; the 90% point uses the thrust and temperature on the left. Mass flow is held fixed (the core turbine nozzle is choked), and the core turbine-exit pressure is solved so the flow passes the necked exits. EGT change is estimated from the change in core-turbine expansion at constant work.
    • Openings. Side windows are where each pipe bore meets the tube wall, with each side capped at its pipe area. They are split at the blade mid-chord: area ahead of it is bypass, area behind it is the exit for gas that went through the wheel. For the end-of-tube and boot layouts, the open tube end is the exit and overhang past the end counts as bypass (the page flags the collision).
    • Flow split. Turbine path = blades in parallel with the tip gap, then the opening behind the wheel. Bypass = the opening ahead of the wheel. Openings lose a full dynamic head at their effective area (discharge coefficient × area), the tip gap has K = 1.2, and the blade flow is found so both paths share the same total-pressure drop.
    • Blades. Axial velocity Vx = blade flow ÷ (ρ × blade passage area, 90% open). Euler work w = U(C − U) with C = Vx(tan α₁ + tan β₂), so U follows from the resistive load: P = ṁ·w = k·U². The blade-row total-pressure drop is ρw + ½ρζW₂² + ½ρζi(W₁ sin i)², where W₂ = Vx/cos β₂ and i is the relative inflow angle minus the blade inlet angle. Without guide vanes the gas meets the blades about 90° off their design inlet angle, which is why incidence matters.
    • Guide vanes add swirl α₁ (raising C and the speed), a loss of Kv ÷ cos²α₁ vane-exit dynamic heads, and their own wake forcing at the vane count.
    • Duct losses (handbook-range estimates): hub cavity, struts, vanes or spinner on the engine-exit dynamic head; the turn into the pipes (side windows, 0.6 × (1 − cos θ) sharp or 0.3 flared, on the pipe head); end cap 0.12 flat or 0.03 with a cone splitter; tee, expansion and bracket terms for the end and boot layouts; return bends; friction; neck discharge coefficient.
    • Uneven loading. Back-pressure swing at the split = q × (0.15 + 0.85 × (1 − cos θ)) × layout factors, decayed as e−2L/r over the settling distance, compared with the blade-row pressure drop. Strut or vane wakes add their own swing, and k × swing² of the work is lost.
    • Not modelled: compressibility within the split (ρ is taken just ahead of the wheel); swirl in the bypass gas; the motor's winding resistance and inductance, which cut voltage at 1.5–2 kHz; heat loss; changes in engine mass flow. Loss coefficients are estimates, so differences between designs are more reliable than absolute numbers.

    8. What to measure on Ben's CAD

    Enter these on the left under Ben's layout, then press Set load for 1000 W and Size exits for EGT target.

    • Inlet tube ID and where its end cap sits.
    • Wheel leading-edge position at the nominal setting, and the slot travel either way.
    • Pipe OD and wall, the angle the pipes leave the tube wall at, and the bend centreline radius.
    • Where each pipe's centreline crosses the tube wall (the window centre), and the gap between the legs at the motor box.
    • Exit ID after necking and the neck cone angle.
    • The hub cone and strut sizes (blue markups), and the bearing bracket's size behind the wheel.
    • Off the CAD: ping-test a blade on the spare wheel for its first mode, and measure the blade exit angle.
