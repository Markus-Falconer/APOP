The idea

Ben's layout is built for easy fabrication. A straight tube bolts to the nozzle flange and the spare turbine wheel slides inside it. Two bent pipes join the tube through windows cut in its side wall, then turn back aft and neck down at the exits. The tube carries on past the windows to an end cap, and a flat-plate box behind the cap carries the motor and a slotted bearing mount. Because the bore is continuous, the wheel can slide over the windows without hitting anything; joining the pipes at the end of the tube doesn't allow that, because the 2 in pipes are smaller than the 58 mm wheel. This page models those specifics on top of the V2 engine and wheel model:

  • Window bypass. As the blade row slides over the windows, the window area beside it opens a bypass path. The windows are elliptical, so bypass ramps up over about 10 mm of travel rather than all at once.
  • Split losses. The turn into the side windows, sharp or flared window edges, the flat end cap or a cone splitter on it, the sudden expansion into the big pipes, the return bends and the necked exits.
  • Uneven loading. The split's back-pressure pattern, plus any strut wakes, swing the blade loading twice per revolution. That costs wheel efficiency and sets the forcing frequencies to check against the blade's first mode.
  • Hub treatment. An open cavity between the engine hub and the wheel, a hub cone on struts, or a spinner on the wheel.

1. Three versions in 3D

Ben's CAD as drawn, and two slightly optimized versions built from the same kinds of parts. Each model is built from that version's inputs, so it updates as you edit. Cutaway removes the top half; Exploded pulls the parts apart.

Ben's layoutpipes joined on the tube sides, necked exits

  • Straight 60 mm tube (1 mm tip gap) that runs past the pipes to a flat end cap; the bearing housing passes through the cap into the motor box.
  • 2 in pipes leave the side wall at 60° and bend back at 1.5D. Their windows run from about 23 to 77 mm aft of the flange, and the wheel sits where its blades just overlap them.
  • Hub cone on two struts (blue markups), sharp window edges, exits necked to give a +30 K EGT rise.

V3-1 Fairedside windows, cleaned up

  • Same side-window layout, with the window edges flared or filleted (the red markups) so gas turns into the pipes without separating.
  • A sheet-metal cone splitter on the end cap steers the gas outward into the windows instead of letting it pile up on a flat face.
  • Pipes leave at 45° instead of 60°, which brings the legs in by about 16 mm per side.
  • Spinner on the wheel instead of the strut-mounted cone, so there are no wakes upstream of the blades.

V3-2 Cone + gentle splitcone shroud, 35° legs

  • A rolled 8° cone replaces the straight tube, so the blades stay shrouded and bypass changes smoothly across the travel.
  • The split starts about 19 mm behind the wheel, which lets the back-pressure pattern die out before it reaches the blades. Keep the wheel inside the cone: the boot narrows to the pipe height, so past the cone's end the tips would hit it.
  • Legs leave at 35° with 1.5D bends; 1¾ in pipes and a 36 mm gap pull the width in.
  • Spinner and rounded bearing fairing as in V3-1.

4. Where the pressure goes ·

Total-pressure drop at 90% speed, from the engine's turbine exit to the nozzle exits. With fixed exits, duct loss shows up as engine back-pressure (hotter EGT); after resizing the exits for the same EGT, it shows up as lost thrust.

5. Uneven loading and blade forcing

The split's back-pressure pattern repeats twice per revolution, so each blade loads and unloads at 2× running speed, with harmonics at 4×, 6× and 8×. Struts add their own orders. The wheel carries only about 5% of the torque it carries in the engine, so the swing costs efficiency more than blade life. A resonance would change that, which is what the chart checks.

7. How this model differs from Aft Turbine V2

  • Same engine and wheel core. Stock nozzle area back-calculated from published thrust and mass flow; Euler work on the wheel with a uniform axial velocity; a fixed load resistor sets the speed; the core turbine-exit pressure is solved so the flow passes the exits, and EGT change is estimated from the change in core-turbine expansion.
  • Window bypass. The window shape comes from where each pipe bore meets the tube wall. The window area alongside the blade row (k × area; k is the window effectiveness) is added to the flow area at the wheel, with a mixing loss of 0.25 × (open area ÷ blade outer area) dynamic heads. For the end-of-tube layout, overhang past the end counts as k × π × D × overhang, and the page flags the collision.
  • Split losses, on the dynamic head just behind the wheel: the turn into the legs, K = (0.08 + 1 − cos θ) for legs leaving at θ (× 0.75 with flared side windows, × 0.55 with a faired boot); window edges 0.10 sharp or 0.03 flared; Borda–Carnot for the sudden expansion into the legs (× 0.6 flared, × 0.35 boot); 0.12 for a flat end cap or bracket, 0.03 with a cone splitter or rounded fairing.
  • Leg losses, on the pipe dynamic head: return bends (θ ÷ 90°) × (0.13 + 0.12 ÷ (R/D)^1.2), and friction f = 0.02.
  • Hub, on the engine-exit dynamic head: 0.15 for an open cavity, 0.03 + 0.035 per strut for a cone on struts, 0.04 for a spinner.
  • Necked exits. Discharge coefficient 0.985 − 0.085 × (half-angle ÷ 45°)^1.3, so a steep neck flows less than its diameter suggests.
  • Uneven loading. Back-pressure swing at the split = q × (0.15 + 0.85 × (1 − cos θ)), × 0.6 with flared windows or 0.45 with a boot, × 1.15 with a flat end cap or bracket, then decayed as e−2L/r over the settling distance L. That swing is compared with the pressure drop across the blades, strut wakes are added, and wheel efficiency becomes η × (1 − k × swing²).
  • All loss coefficients are handbook-range estimates, not measurements; treat differences between versions as more reliable than the absolute numbers. CFD or a back-to-back test should replace them.

8. What to measure on Ben's CAD

Enter these on the left under Ben's layout, then press Set load for 1000 W and Size exits for EGT target.

  • Inlet tube ID and where its end cap sits.
  • Wheel leading-edge position at the nominal setting, and the slot travel either way.
  • Pipe OD and wall, the angle the pipes leave the tube wall at, and the bend centreline radius.
  • Where each pipe's centreline crosses the tube wall (the window centre), and the gap between the legs at the motor box.
  • Exit ID after necking and the neck cone angle.
  • The hub cone and strut sizes (blue markups), and the bearing bracket's size behind the wheel.
  • Off the CAD: ping-test a blade on the spare wheel for its first mode, and measure the blade exit angle.
