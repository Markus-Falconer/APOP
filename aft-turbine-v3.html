The idea

Ben's layout is built for easy fabrication. A short straight tube bolts to the nozzle flange, and the spare turbine wheel sits at its open end. Bypass is set by how far the wheel hangs past the end of the tube (the lip). Two bent pipes leave almost sideways right behind the wheel, then turn back aft and neck down at the exits. A flat-plate box between the pipes carries the motor and a slotted bearing bracket. This page models those specifics on top of the V2 engine and wheel model:

  • Lip bypass. Blade length past the lip opens a side window that bypasses gas, so moving the wheel does nothing until the trailing edge reaches the lip, then changes power quickly.
  • Tee split right behind the wheel. Losses from the abrupt split, the sudden expansion into the big pipes, the flat bracket, the return bends and the necked exits.
  • Uneven loading. The split's back-pressure pattern, plus any strut wakes, swing the blade loading twice per revolution. That costs wheel efficiency and sets the forcing frequencies to check against the blade's first mode.
  • Hub treatment. An open cavity between the engine hub and the wheel, a hub cone on struts, or a spinner on the wheel.

As drawnBen's CAD, with necked exits

  • Straight 60 mm tube with the wheel at its lip; the trailing edge hangs about 2 mm past the lip for bypass.
  • 2 in pipes leave at about 65° right behind the wheel, with 2D bends back to parallel; the tube's end wall closes the top and bottom.
  • Hub cone on two struts (blue markups), flat bearing bracket in the crotch, exits necked to give a +30 K EGT rise.

V3-1 Fairedsame parts, cleaned up

  • Red-markup fairings replace the tube's end wall with a smooth sheet-metal boot into the legs.
  • Elbows trimmed so the legs leave at 50°, which brings them in by about 8 mm per side.
  • Spinner on the wheel instead of the strut-mounted cone (no wakes), and a rounded bearing fairing instead of the flat bracket.
  • Keeps Ben's lip bypass and wheel position, so the adjustment works the same way.

V3-2 Cone + gentle splitcone shroud, 35° legs

  • A rolled 8° cone replaces the straight tube, so the blades stay shrouded and bypass changes smoothly across the travel.
  • The split starts about 19 mm behind the wheel, which lets the back-pressure pattern die out before it reaches the blades.
  • Legs leave at 35° with 1.5D bends; 1¾ in pipes and a 36 mm gap pull the width in.
  • Spinner and rounded bearing fairing as in V3-1.

4. Where the pressure goes ·

Total-pressure drop at 90% speed, from the engine's turbine exit to the nozzle exits. With fixed exits, duct loss shows up as engine back-pressure (hotter EGT); after resizing the exits for the same EGT, it shows up as lost thrust.

5. Uneven loading and blade forcing

The split's back-pressure pattern repeats twice per revolution, so each blade loads and unloads at 2× running speed, with harmonics at 4×, 6× and 8×. Struts add their own orders. The wheel carries only about 5% of the torque it carries in the engine, so the swing costs efficiency more than blade life. A resonance would change that, which is what the chart checks.

7. How this model differs from Aft Turbine V2

  • Same engine and wheel core. Stock nozzle area back-calculated from published thrust and mass flow; Euler work on the wheel with a uniform axial velocity; a fixed load resistor sets the speed; the core turbine-exit pressure is solved so the flow passes the exits, and EGT change is estimated from the change in core-turbine expansion.
  • Lip bypass. Overhang past the lip adds an open area of k × π × D × overhang beside the blades (k is the lip-window effectiveness), plus a mixing loss of 0.25 × (overhang ÷ blade width) dynamic heads.
  • Split losses, on the dynamic head just behind the wheel: a tee or Y with K = (0.08 + 1 − cos θ) for legs leaving at θ, × 0.55 with fairings; Borda–Carnot for the sudden expansion into the legs, × 0.35 with fairings; 0.12 for a flat bracket in the crotch or 0.03 rounded.
  • Leg losses, on the pipe dynamic head: return bends (θ ÷ 90°) × (0.13 + 0.12 ÷ (R/D)^1.2), and friction f = 0.02.
  • Hub, on the engine-exit dynamic head: 0.15 for an open cavity, 0.03 + 0.035 per strut for a cone on struts, 0.04 for a spinner.
  • Necked exits. Discharge coefficient 0.985 − 0.085 × (half-angle ÷ 45°)^1.3, so a steep neck flows less than its diameter suggests.
  • Uneven loading. Back-pressure swing at the split = q × (0.15 + 0.85 × (1 − cos θ)), × 0.45 with fairings and × 1.15 with a flat bracket, then decayed as e−2L/r over the settling distance L. That swing is compared with the pressure drop across the blades, strut wakes are added, and wheel efficiency becomes η × (1 − k × swing²).
  • All loss coefficients are handbook-range estimates, not measurements; treat differences between versions as more reliable than the absolute numbers. CFD or a back-to-back test should replace them.

8. What to measure on Ben's CAD

Enter these on the left under As drawn, then press Set load for 1000 W and Size exits for EGT target.

  • Inlet tube ID and its length from the nozzle flange to the lip.
  • Wheel leading-edge position at the nominal setting, and the slot travel either way.
  • Pipe OD and wall, the angle the legs leave at, and the bend centreline radius.
  • Where the legs meet relative to the tube end, and the gap between the legs at the motor box.
  • Exit ID after necking and the neck cone angle.
  • The hub cone and strut sizes (blue markups), and the bearing bracket's size behind the wheel.
  • Off the CAD: ping-test a blade on the spare wheel for its first mode, and measure the blade exit angle.
