Guide vanes

A fixed vane row between the engine turbine exit and the wheel. It pre-swirls the exhaust, which raises the work the wheel can do for a given axial velocity.

The idea

The stock nozzle comes off and a bolt-on module takes its place. A copy of the engine's turbine wheel sits in a cone shroud right behind the core turbine and drives a Lehner 1535 motor on the same axis through a magnetic coupler. The exhaust splits left and right around the motor into two round pipes that end in plain round exits. Sliding the wheel aft in the cone opens the gap over the blade tips, which bypasses gas and slows the wheel. Every output below is a first-pass 1-D estimate; replace the baseline numbers with test data as they arrive.

4. How the estimate works

  • Baseline. The stock nozzle's effective area is back-calculated from full-throttle thrust, mass flow and exhaust temperature. The 90% point uses that area with the 90% thrust and temperature you enter.
  • Wheel. Work per kilogram is w = U·(C − U) with C = Vx·(tan α₁ + tan β₂). Vx is the axial velocity through the blades plus the tip gap, taken as uniform. The share of gas inside the blade annulus does work; the rest bypasses.
  • Load. The load bank is a fixed resistor, so electrical power is V²/R with V = rpm ÷ Kv. That sets where the wheel settles.
  • Guide vanes. With vanes on, α₁ is the vane angle, and the vane loss is charged to the whole flow. Vanes raise the runaway speed and the power ceiling, so the wheel can sit further aft with more bypass. At this light loading the gas still meets the blades about 45° off their design inlet angle with or without vanes, so the default efficiency gain is small (0.50 to 0.55); change it if testing shows more.
  • Engine match. The round exits have a fixed area. The model finds the core turbine-exit pressure that pushes the flow through the vanes, cone, wheel, pipes and exits, then estimates the EGT change from the smaller or larger expansion across the core turbine at constant speed. This step is the roughest part of the page.
  • Round pipes. Two round pipes beside a 38 mm tunnel are wider than the engine case, which costs radial-distance points. The plan section and score table show how much.
