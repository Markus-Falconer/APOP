1. What the ICD sets for the competition load bank

  • Your system delivers 1–200 V DC into a facility DC load bank.
  • Voltage ripple should be under about 20% of the supplied voltage. No filtering is needed with a commercially available 3-phase rectifier, but it must cope with a fast-spinning generator.
  • The load bank has constant current (CC), constant voltage (CV), constant resistance (CR) and constant power (CP) modes, and you pick the one that suits your generator.
  • It has a male EC5 plug, so your side needs a female EC5.
  • The rectifier counts as support equipment: it goes in your sealed support box and is reviewed at Milestone 4.

Your own test setup should copy that interface: a DC load with the same four modes, behind a female-to-male EC5 joint.

3. Recommendation

  • Borrow or rent first. Ask OSU's power electronics and senior-design labs, and test-equipment rental companies. You only need a 1.5 kW programmable load for a few months.
  • If you buy one, a 1.5 kW, 500 V unit like the ITECH IT8514B+ covers every design being considered. Two 750 W, 500 V loads (B&K 8612) in parallel also work.
  • Build a resistor bank as well. Size it for your bus voltage (section 6), make it switchable in stages, and add a DC power meter and a scope. Use it as a backup load, an overspeed dump, and the load for early bench runs.
  • Ask the APOP team which load bank model they use. Then you can check its minimum voltage and how each mode behaves with your generator before test day.

4. What to look for in a load bank

  1. Power: at least 1.5 kW continuous at your operating voltage, so you can test past 1 kW. Read the power-vs-voltage curve.
  2. Voltage: rated above your worst-case open-circuit voltage, including overspeed and no-load runs, with margin. A 500 V-class load covers the whole ICD range; a 120–150 V unit is fine only if your bus stays under about 100 V.
  3. Current: I = P/V at your bus voltage (top left).
  4. Minimum operating voltage: 500 V-class loads need a few volts to draw full current (3.6 V at 60 A on the B&K 8616). It only matters for very low-voltage designs.
  5. All four modes: CC, CV, CR and CP (CP is sometimes called CW). They behave differently with a generator:
    • CR is the most forgiving: power is V²/R, and with the speed held, voltage just follows speed. Start here.
    • CV works like a speed setpoint for a free aft rotor. On a front-shaft generator it can demand too much current.
    • CC holds generator torque constant. Stable for the aft rotor as long as the voltage stays above the load's minimum.
    • CP is the least forgiving: if the generator can't supply the set power, voltage collapses. Try it last.
  6. Stability with an inductive source: the generator's windings and the load's control loop can oscillate. An adjustable slew rate or CV loop speed helps, and so does a capacitor on the DC bus.
  7. Logging: USB or LAN with SCPI commands, so you can script 30-second averages like the competition scoring.
  8. Protections: over-voltage, over-current, over-power, over-temperature and reverse polarity.
  9. Practical points: fan noise and size, runs on 120 V mains, terminals you can adapt to a male EC5, light enough to carry to engine runs.

5. The rest of the test kit

Power path

Generator → 3-phase rectifier → DC fuse or breaker → female EC5 → load bank.

  • Rectifier: a 3-phase bridge with heatsinking, rated well above your current and above the highest open-circuit voltage you could ever see, including overspeed. Fast-recovery or Schottky diodes handle the electrical frequency comfortably (top right). An unfiltered 3-phase bridge dips to about 87% of peak, so ripple is about 13% before any capacitor: that passes the ICD's 20%, but measure it.
  • Fuse or breaker: rated for DC at your maximum voltage. DC arcs are much harder to break than AC.
  • Wiring: short, heavy cable sized for at least 1.5× full-load current, with strain relief.

Measurement

  • The load's own voltage, current and power readback, logged to a PC.
  • An independent DC power meter or current shunt to cross-check the load.
  • An oscilloscope for ripple. If the DC bus isn't ground-referenced, use a differential or isolated probe.
  • Generator speed (optical or magnetic pickup) and thermocouples on the stator, rectifier and load.
  • A way to line the electrical log up in time with the ECU log and thrust.

Safety

  • An emergency stop, ideally a DC contactor.
  • An independent overspeed dump resistor. Essential for the aft turbine, which has no governor and runs away if the load drops.
  • An enclosure around live terminals, a Class C-rated fire extinguisher, and eye protection.

Bench dyno

Drive the generator with a motor before it goes on the engine, so the load and rectifier are proven first.

6. Resistor bank sizing

A fixed resistor bank is CR mode only. Stages switched in parallel give steps of load; values at your bus voltage.
