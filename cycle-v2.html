Calibration to test data

Each test point is run backwards through the model: the measured nozzle, thrust and EGT give the air flow, turbine inlet state and turbine efficiency. Edit the values or add points as you run more tests.

Fuel flow is optional. The full-throttle value is JetCat's 390 ml/min spec, not a measurement. It only sets the combustion efficiency, so it changes the predicted fuel flow and TSFC but not thrust or EGT.

How this model works

  • Compressor. Work comes from tip speed, Δh₀ = ψU², using your 60 mm impeller. ψ is set so the pressure ratio is JetCat's 2.9 at 154,000 rpm, so pressure ratio rises with speed squared. Air flow follows corrected speed as m ∝ Nk, with k fitted from the test points. Under load the compressor can slide along its speed line, r ∝ m−S: lower flow and higher pressure ratio, toward surge.
  • Combustor. Liquid n-dodecane at ambient temperature, complete lean combustion. A combustion efficiency (which also covers heat lost through the casing) comes from the fuel flow, plus a pressure-loss fraction.
  • Turbine. Drives the compressor plus the generator shaft power (electrical ÷ efficiency, plus any extra drag). Bearing losses are folded into the calibrated turbine efficiency. The nozzle guide vanes are treated as choked, so m·√T₀₃/P₀₃ is fixed. The two test points agree on this value to within about 1.3%, which supports the assumption.
  • Nozzle. Your measured 47.62 mm exit (minus any tail cone at the exit plane) with a discharge coefficient. The NGV and nozzle together fix the operating point: the model solves for the air flow and fuel flow that satisfy both.
  • Corrected values. θ = Tamb/288.15 K and δ = Pamb/101.325 kPa. Corrected speed is N/√θ and corrected thrust is F/δ. The 90% scored condition is 138,600 rpm corrected, about 139,000 rpm in the scoring criteria.
  • Thermodynamics. Ideal-gas mixtures with NASA 7-coefficient polynomials (GRI-Mech 3.0) for N₂, O₂, CO₂ and H₂O, which are variable-property throughout.

Limits of the calibration

  • Both test points are at 97–100% speed. Predictions at 90% speed are an extrapolation of about 10% in speed; a run at 138,600 rpm will tighten them more than anything else.
  • Fuel flow was not measured. That leaves predicted fuel flow and TSFC uncertain, but not thrust or EGT.
  • The speed-line slope S is a guess without a compressor map. It hardly affects results with the stock nozzle, but it matters once the nozzle diameter is changed from the tested one.
  • ηc, ηn, Cd and the pressure ratio are assumed. Each one trades against the calibrated turbine efficiency and air flow, so the predictions barely move when you change them. Changing them shifts the fitted values instead.

Next tests that would help most

  • Hold 138,600 rpm and log thrust and EGT. Enter it as a third test point.
  • Measure fuel flow at each point. Weigh the tank before and after a timed hold, or use the ECU's logged consumption if it records one.
  • Check whether the inner cone reaches the nozzle exit plane. If it does, enter its diameter there.
